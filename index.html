Black Holes

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing—not even light—can escape. Predicted by Einstein's General Relativity (1915), first imaged by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019 (M87*) and 2022 (Sagittarius A*).

Key Theories

  • Schwarzschild Radius: The event horizon radius r = 2GM/c².
  • Hawking Radiation: Black holes slowly evaporate via quantum effects.
  • Information Paradox: Is information lost when matter falls in?

The Big Bang

The universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state. Evidence: cosmic microwave background (CMB), redshift of galaxies, abundance of light elements.

Dark Matter & Dark Energy

~27% of the universe is dark matter, ~68% is dark energy, and only ~5% is ordinary matter. Dark matter is inferred from galaxy rotation curves (Vera Rubin, 1970s) and gravitational lensing.
